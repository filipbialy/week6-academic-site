Research

My research looks at the politics of digital technologies: who shapes them, who benefits from them, and how they could be governed differently.

Digital capitalism

How digital platforms and artificial intelligence reshape economic and political power, and what ethical, political and ideological resources we have to challenge them. This is the subject of my book in preparation.

AI literacy

What it means to understand artificial intelligence, and whose interests AI literacy programmes serve. My current work examines AI literacy in the Global South, between digital upskilling and technocolonialism.

Generative AI in higher education

How universities respond to generative AI. With colleagues, I have mapped the policies of UK higher education institutions and found that most frame AI as a problem of academic misconduct rather than of teaching and learning.

Data governance

How researchers can responsibly link survey data with social media data. This work develops a governance framework that balances research value against the risk of identifying participants.

Data-driven campaigning

How political parties use data and online advertising, and whether they reach the audiences they intend to reach. This work draws on ad archives and survey data.

Democracy and technology

What democratic theory, and agonistic democracy in particular, can tell us about conflict and disagreement over digital technologies. This was the focus of my doctoral research.
